01 — The Story So Far

A short history of the NBA

The league's identity has shifted with every generation of players who reshaped how the game is played. Here's the arc, decade by decade.

1946 — The BAA is founded

Eleven arena owners in the Northeast and Midwest formed the Basketball Association of America, betting that pro basketball could fill arenas on nights hockey wasn't playing.

1949 — The NBA is born

The BAA merged with the rival National Basketball League to form the National Basketball Association, unifying the sport's top talent under one league for the first time.

1950s-60s — The Russell era

Bill Russell's Celtics won 11 titles in 13 seasons, built on a defensive philosophy that proved championships could be won without the league's leading scorer.

1976 — The ABA merger

Four American Basketball Association teams joined the NBA, bringing the three-point line, the slam dunk contest's spirit, and a faster, more athletic style with them.

1980s — Magic, Bird, and TV

The Lakers-Celtics rivalry, carried by Magic Johnson and Larry Bird, coincided with the NBA's first prime-time network deals and turned the league into a national obsession.

1990s — The Jordan era

Michael Jordan's Bulls won six titles in eight years and turned the NBA into a global product, expanding the league's reach well beyond North America.

2000s-2010s — Pace and space

Analytics-driven offenses prioritized three-point shooting and ball movement over traditional post play, led by teams like the Warriors' dynasty of the mid-2010s.

Today — A global, positionless game

Modern rosters blend size and skill at every position, drawing talent from every continent and playing a faster, more perimeter-oriented style than any prior era.
